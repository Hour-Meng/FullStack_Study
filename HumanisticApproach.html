What Is the Humanistic Approach?

Humanistic psychology emerged as a "third force" — rejecting the determinism of psychoanalysis and behaviorism. It centers on human potential, free will, and the drive toward self-actualization.

Rogers

The Self-Concept & Congruence

Carl Rogers argued that personality is shaped by the phenomenal field — our subjective experience of reality. Mental health depends on the alignment between who we are and who we aspire to be.

Neural imaging reveals brain regions associated with self-referential processing

Congruence Model

Real Self Ideal Self Congruence

Congruence

Occurs when the ideal self closely matches the real self, producing psychological well-being. The greater the gap, the more anxiety and insecurity a person experiences.

Unconditional Positive Regard

Rogers believed that children who receive unconditional acceptance develop greater congruence and move toward self-actualization — without conditions of worth distorting their self-concept.

Incongruence

When a large gap exists between real and ideal self, individuals experience anxiety, defensiveness, and psychological distress. Therapy aims to reduce this gap through empathetic understanding.

Empirical research supports the link between self-concept clarity and psychological well-being

Rogers

Characteristics of the Good Life

Rogers described the fully functioning person as someone living authentically and openly. The "good life" is a direction, not a destination.

The good life is a process, not a state of being. It is a direction, not a destination. — Carl Rogers, 1961

Openness to Experience

Receptivity to feelings and new experiences without defensiveness.

Existential Lifestyle

Living fully in each moment rather than rigidly adhering to the past.

Organismic Trust

Trusting one's own inner experience as a reliable guide for behavior.

Freedom of Choice

Taking responsibility for decisions and accepting their consequences.

Creativity

Adapting creatively to new situations with spontaneity and richness.

A Rich, Full Life

The ultimate outcome — experiencing life with depth, intensity, and meaning. Not a static state, but an ongoing process of becoming.
